# Competitor source notes

Read-only research worker findings. Parent recommendations and current-app gaps are in [competitor research](competitor-research.md). These are docs and source checks, not a hands-on product test.

## Recommendation

Copy **interaction polish, not competitor product breadth**. The best additions are recovery, simpler recording gestures, and predictable text corrections—not meetings, analytics, agents, or a sprawling mode system.

**Evidence standard:** “Documented shipped” means official operational documentation or a versioned release description supports availability; I did not runtime-test competitors. “Claim” means marketing describes it without sufficient implementation detail. Research used `tvly` search/extract; its keyless rate cap prevented further extraction, so remaining official pages were read directly. No files edited.

## Eight concrete micro-features

| Micro-feature | Official evidence and status | Lean OpenWispr version | Downsides / relative complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Last-result rescue: Copy, Insert again, Retry** | **Documented shipped — Wispr Flow.** Its [recovery documentation](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4841123325-longer-dictation-sessions-now-up-to-20-minutes) describes **Insert** after microphone disconnection, **Retry** after transcription failure, and **Undo** after cancellation. Recovery inserts into the currently focused field. | Keep one recent result; expose **Copy / Insert again** on the recording indicator. On failure, offer **Retry** without requiring another recording. No history dashboard necessary. | **Low–medium.** Text reinsertion is cheap; retry requires retaining audio temporarily. Explain retention and avoid inserting into an unintended field. Flow explicitly warns that a crash mid-recording is not recovered automatically. |
| **2. One trigger: hold to dictate, tap for hands-free** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** [Essential settings](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/essential-settings) says Push to Talk records while held, stops on release, and a quick tap acts as toggle. | One shortcut with both gestures; tapping again stops hands-free recording. Android can use the same tap/hold distinction on its microphone button. | **Medium.** Distinguishing tap from hold must feel immediate. Desktop shortcut/key-release behavior depends on the platform; Android touch handling differs. Avoid adding several overlapping shortcuts. |
| **3. Tiny, unmistakable recording feedback** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** Its [recording window](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-rec-window) shows a live microphone waveform and stop/cancel controls. Its [menu-bar indicator](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-menu-bar) distinguishes loading, recording, processing, and completion. [Sound settings](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/essential-settings) provide independently adjustable start/stop cues. | A compact indicator with **Listening / Processing / Done**, a small input-level meter, and optional quiet start/stop sounds. Reuse existing UI rather than opening another window. | **Low–medium.** Waveforms can become decorative noise. Do not depend on color alone; sounds must be disableable. A simple level meter is enough. |
| **4. Deterministic spoken replacements and tiny snippets** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** [Vocabulary documentation](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-vocabulary) says replacements run programmatically after transcription, **“with no AI interpretation.”** Examples include “my work email” → an address and “at sign” → `@`. **Willow claim:** its [dictation page](https://willowvoice.com/features/dictation) advertises saved phrases inserted with a simple command. | A two-column **Heard phrase → Output** list. Handle persistent name errors, email addresses, and a few frequently used phrases through one mechanism. | **Low–medium.** Broad triggers can replace unintended prose. Use clearly defined matching and explicit uncommon snippet triggers. A snippet library, folders, sharing, or sync would be unnecessary scope. |
| **5. Small, explicit personal vocabulary** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** [Official docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-vocabulary) explain that vocabulary words are recognition hints and warn that too many can hurt accuracy. **Willow claim:** its [dictation page](https://willowvoice.com/features/dictation) advertises an auto-learning dictionary remembering names, product terms, and abbreviations. | Let users add names and technical terms manually; ideally offer **Remember this spelling** when reviewing a correction. Keep this distinct from guaranteed post-transcription replacements. | **Medium; backend-dependent.** Recognition hints are not guarantees. Large lists can affect language detection and punctuation. **Do not copy automatic learning first:** correction detection, mistaken learning, and privacy add hidden complexity. |
| **6. Preserve the user’s clipboard after dictation** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** [Advanced settings](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings-advanced) includes **Restore clipboard**, restoring previous contents after pasting, with a configurable delay. | Where desktop insertion relies on clipboard pasting, preserve the existing clipboard instead of silently replacing something the user copied. Android keyboard insertion should use its text-input connection rather than emulate desktop clipboard delivery. | **Medium, platform-sensitive.** Restoration can race with slow pasting or a fresh user copy; rich clipboard formats complicate preservation. This belongs in the insertion layer, not a new UI surface. |
| **7. Fast cancel, with protection for long recordings** | **Documented shipped — Superwhisper.** Its [recording-window docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-rec-window) say Escape/cancel discards short recordings immediately, while recordings **over 30 seconds require confirmation**. Wispr’s recovery docs additionally describe cancellation Undo. | Immediate cancellation for accidental short starts; protect substantial recordings with either confirmation or a temporary **Undo discard** action. | **Low–medium.** Confirmation interrupts flow; Undo requires temporary retention. Choose one protection, not both layered together. Never silently throw away a long recording. |
| **8. Selection-aware voice correction** | **Documented shipped — Aqua Voice.** Its [Edit Mode guide](https://aquavoice.com/edit-mode) describes selecting text, holding the normal dictation key, and speaking a change. A chip shows **“12 words selected”**; commands include “undo that.” Its [official overview](https://aquavoice.com/llms.txt) identifies desktop release **v0.17.0, July 20, 2026**. | On supported surfaces, detect a selection and visibly offer **Replace selected text** using the existing dictation trigger. Start with literal replacement before open-ended AI rewriting. | **High cross-platform.** Selection access and replacement vary between editors and operating systems. Automatic mode changes can surprise users. Full rewrite instructions and multi-step voice undo are **not** a first-wave micro-feature. |

## Opinionated top three

1. **Last-result rescue.** Highest trust payoff: failed insertion or transcription should not force someone to repeat their thoughts. Start with one-result **Copy / Insert again**; add audio retry only where straightforward.
2. **One tap/hold trigger.** Makes frequent use easier without expanding the interface. Particularly attractive as a consistent interaction across desktop and the Android keyboard.
3. **Deterministic replacements.** Fixes recurring personal annoyances predictably, without another model, subscription dependency, or elaborate settings system.

**Close fourth:** clipboard preservation—it is excellent invisible polish where clipboard-based insertion is already used.

**Defer:** automatic vocabulary learning, arbitrary app-specific style rules, and full selection-based AI editing. They look small in marketing but introduce substantially more state, permissions, and surprising behavior. Willow’s auto-learning/personal-style descriptions should be treated as **claims, not verified mechanics**.
